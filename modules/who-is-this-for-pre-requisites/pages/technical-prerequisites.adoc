This course focuses on the internal mechanics of Kubernetes extensibility—custom controllers, admission webhooks, and distributed state management. To get the most out of it, you should have a solid foundation in container orchestration and be comfortable with the competencies below.

== Recommended Competencies

* *Container Fundamentals:* Images, container lifecycles, and basic networking (e.g. `podman` or `docker` workflows).
* *Kubernetes Basics:* Standard resources (Deployments, Services, ConfigMaps), the `kubectl` CLI, and basic RBAC (Roles, RoleBindings).
* *Distributed Systems:* A working knowledge of eventual consistency, concurrency and race conditions, and asynchronous, event-driven processing.
* *Programming:* Because we interact with `client-go` and implement controllers, proficiency in *Go (Golang)*—interfaces, goroutines/channels, and error handling—is highly recommended.

== Ideal Baseline

We recommend that participants meet one of the following benchmarks:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Benchmark | Description

| *CKAD Certification* | Hands-on proficiency in designing, building, and deploying cloud-native applications on Kubernetes.
| *Red Hat DO-180* | Introduction to Containers, Kubernetes, and Red Hat OpenShift.
| *Red Hat DO-280* | Red Hat OpenShift Administration: managing enterprise-grade Kubernetes clusters.
|===

[NOTE]
====
If you do not hold these credentials, equivalent practical experience is fine. You should be comfortable with the `kubectl` CLI, YAML manifest management, and the fundamental concepts of distributed systems.
====

[TIP]
====
If your knowledge in these areas feels rusty, revisit the official Kubernetes documentation on https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/[Cluster Architecture, window=_blank] and, if needed, work through the https://go.dev/doc/tutorial/[Go Tutorial, window=_blank] before starting the `client-go` lab exercises.
====
